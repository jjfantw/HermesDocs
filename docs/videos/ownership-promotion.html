先判斷後果，再決定是否介入

3:17

影片明確說明，Ownership 並不等於接下所有掉落的工作。面對責任縫隙時，第一個問題不是「這是不是我的事」，而是「如果沒有人處理，後果會是什麼」。

若後果不嚴重，可以不接；若後果嚴重、又沒有其他人承接，才是適合站出來的情況。影片中的示範說法也保留了三個月後重新決定正式歸屬的退出機制。

1:32 Actually, I was the one who miscalculated. In foreign firms, the word "Ownership" is often translated into Chinese as "accountability" or "initiative," but I don't think either is quite right, because its true meaning is closer to: "This task has no owner, so I will claim it." The focus is on the act of "claiming" it, not just finishing the task.

2:08 So, what is your manager actually looking for? How many things have you done that no one asked you to do? This number is the basis for evaluating whether you can handle a bigger role, because it maps to something companies care deeply about: scope. How large is the territory you are responsible for? Scope is directly linked to your job band. Your job band isn't based on how good you are at doing tasks, but on how large an area you can manage.

3:17 Ownership doesn't mean picking up every single thing that drops; that’s not ownership, that’s a lack of judgment, and you’ll burn out within three months. The action that is truly rewarded is this: when a task falls through the cracks, your first thought shouldn't be, "Is this mine?" Your thought should be, "If no one takes care of this, what will the consequences be?"

4:07 Someone said in a meeting, "No one is managing this, and I’m not sure if it should fall to me, but it’s going to blow up next month, so I’ll take it on for now, and in three months, we can decide who it should really belong to." This statement contains three things: they stepped up, they pointed out the consequences, and they retained an exit strategy.

5:08 Ownership and being a meddling micromanager are two completely different things. The difference lies in whether the things you take on have a direct connection to what you're already doing. If the tasks you pick up are related to your expertise and adjacent to your territory, that’s called expansion. If the things you pick up are scattered all over the place, that’s just called firefighting.
